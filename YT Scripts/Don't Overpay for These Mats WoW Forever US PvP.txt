The leveling crowd could be your chance to buy cheaper profession materials. 56% of the sampled US PVP characters are level 20 to 35. Here are the materials I would check and the ones I would leave alone. Look at the distribution. This is Classic Armory's November 10th snapshot. About 185,000 US PvP characters counting both factions. The average is 23.7 and the median is 23. The highlighted 20 to 35 range contains over 103,000 characters. Almost 24% are 20 to 24, 18% are 25 to 29, and 14% are 30 to 35. That is the part of the leveling curve I'm watching. Players in those ranges kill humanoids, mind veins, and skin beasts while they quest. If they sell the materials, that can create plenty of supply. It could be a good time to buy what you need for professions. But the census alone does not prove an auction bargain. Start with wool and silk cloth. Think humanoid camps in Hillsrad, Ashenvail, and Duskwood. silk becomes more relevant toward the upper part of this range. [music] Cloth supports tailoring and bandages, so I would price the next section of my crafting plan. Now, for mining, watch tin and iron ore. Tin comes from tin veins, which need 65 mining. [music] Hills, Brad and Ashenale are examples. Iron deposits need 125 mining with places like Thousand Needles and Arothy becoming relevant as players move up. Check the stones, too. Tin veins can give coarse stone and iron deposits can give heavy stone. those feed profession recipes as well. Compare ore with bars before buying and use the recipes actual quantities. A cheap ore price does not automatically make the finished craft cheap. For skinning, put medium and heavy leather on the list. Wetlands raptors are a useful medium leather example. Beasts in Thousand Needles and Stranglethorn bring heavy leather into the picture. These are overlapping source tiers, not exact level cut offs or proof that every character is gathering. Now, check actual auction supply. In the November 11th US PVP horde scan, the site lists roughly 11,000 silk, 5,000 tin, and 4 a half,000 wool. Those are units offered for sale, not completed sales. The item price still has to make sense. Your faction matters. On Alliance, tin is above its recent average while iron is below. The same US PvP population does not mean the same auction price. Set the region, rule set, and faction before comparing anything. My shopping rule is simple. Pick your next recipes, total the materials, and compare their current unit prices with recent prices.
