# What Chip wants from Gold Finder

The yardstick for every change, by any session or agent. Distilled from Chip's original brief and every correction he made while using the app (November 18 and 19).

1. **A money radar for his own houses, now.** Alliance 111 and his Blackwater house, in phase 1 of the economy, where most things trade in silver and a few in gold. Find what he would otherwise stumble on, before it fades.
2. **Real over flashy.** Every number must be believable. A play shows only with at least 1s profit per item (his setting), real sales seen near the price it claims, listings holding that price, and a steady price. A big number on thin evidence is a red flag, never an opportunity. The 76g hammer, one copper flips and troll listings must never reach him.
3. **Readable in three seconds.** Item, the profit per item as the big number, then short lines: Buy (where, price, who, map link), Sell (where, price, map link), Why people buy it (quest or recipe), Price (what is listed, what sold, which price was used and why), Volume (sales a day, what is worth doing, what it costs), List (2h, 8h, 24h with the best bold), and Strength.
4. **Ranked by money he can actually make:** profit per item times a realistic number of units, about 20 percent of daily sales, capped by his gold on hand. Within his reach and skills.
5. **Hands off.** Collect, log, analyze and alert in the background. Discord only when something is worth it: plays and dev news in separate channels. He never updates anything by hand.
6. **Transparent.** Always say which price was used and why, what was held back and why, and what is not known yet. An empty board explains itself.
7. **Honest data.** Verify before trusting. Never infer a shape from a name. Admit what cannot be known, such as past sales, which no source publishes.
8. **A practical crafting helper.** Per single item numbers, and a leveling plan from the guide that uses what he owns (Mining 145, Blacksmithing 33), treating his materials as free unless selling them is smarter.
9. **Clean, light, plain words.** No jargon (floor, ladder, detector, confidence), no clutter, no confusing totals, no hyphens or em dashes in his text. A light WoW flavor and a map.
10. **It gets smarter.** Calibrate from what really sells, and from his feedback. When he calls something wrong, trace why with evidence before fixing.

## Signals from his corrections
- He spots fake numbers instantly and expects the tool to as well. Skepticism is a feature.
- He thinks in profit per item and units per day, not totals.
- He wants to understand why: why it sells, why this price, why nothing is showing.
- He acts in game quickly: locations, prices to pay and sell at, and travel matter more than charts.
11. **Additive, never subtractive.** A new request is in addition to everything already agreed, never instead of it. Nothing on a page is removed, hidden or pushed down unless Chip asks for that exact change. Agreed content is listed in docs/page-contracts.md and guarded by a test. (November 20: a new recipe table pushed the leveling steps off screen, and Chip lost the plan he relied on.)
